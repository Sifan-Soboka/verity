# Verity demonstration scoring policy (`verity-demo-1.0`)

> **Academic demonstration only.** The weights, reference points and limits below were chosen so the
> formula is easy to explain and reproduces the five illustrative scenarios in the CSIT305 report.
> They are **not** taken from any bank, regulator or credit bureau, and they are **not** SHAP or LIME
> values. No machine-learning model is trained or used.

All values live in one place: [`lib/engine/policy.ts`](../lib/engine/policy.ts). The engine is
[`lib/engine/decision.ts`](../lib/engine/decision.ts); explanations are generated by
[`lib/explain/explanation.ts`](../lib/explain/explanation.ts).

---

## 1. The formula

```
score = 50 + contribution(income) + contribution(DTI) + contribution(credit score)

APPROVED  if  score ≥ 50  AND  DTI ≤ 60%
REJECTED  otherwise
```

Each factor has three **anchor points** and a **maximum number of points** it can add or remove:

| Factor | Full concern at | Reference (0 pts) | Full support at | Max points |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Monthly income (AED) | 2,000 | 6,000 | 15,000 | ±10 |
| Debt-to-income ratio (%) — *lower is better* | 70 | 30 | 10 | ±14 |
| Credit score | 550 | 640 | 800 | ±26 |

Because the maxima add up to 50, the score always lies between **0 and 100**.

### Step 1 — normalise each value to −1 … +1

```
if the value is on the "support" side of the reference:
    n = min(1, |value − reference| / |fullSupportAt − reference|)
otherwise:
    n = −min(1, |value − reference| / |fullConcernAt − reference|)
```

So a value at the reference point gives 0, a value at (or beyond) the support anchor gives +1, and a
value at (or beyond) the concern anchor gives −1. Between anchors the effect changes in a straight line.

### Step 2 — contribution in points

```
contribution = round₁(maxPoints × n)          (rounded half away from zero to 1 decimal place)
```

Contributions are rounded **before** they are added, so the numbers on screen always add up exactly
to the displayed score.

### Step 3 — score and decision

`score = round₁(50 + Σ contributions)`. The application is approved when the score is at least 50
**and** the DTI is not above the 60% affordability limit (see §4).

### Labels used in the explanation

| Contribution | Label |
|---|---|
| ≥ +0.5 points | **Supports approval** |
| ≤ −0.5 points | **Potential concern** |
| between | **Little effect** |

Strength wording uses |n|: ≥ 0.6 *strong*, ≥ 0.25 *moderate*, otherwise *slight*. A score within
5 points of the threshold is described as *close to the threshold* (the report's "borderline" cases).

## 2. Worked examples — the five report scenarios

| | Income | DTI | Credit | Income pts | DTI pts | Credit pts | **Score** | Engine | Report |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| A | 15,000 | 18% | 790 | +10.0 | +8.4 | +24.4 | **92.8** | Approved | Approved ✅ |
| B | 10,000 | 35% | 720 | +4.4 | −1.8 | +13.0 | **65.6** | Approved | Approved ✅ |
| C | 8,000 | 48% | 690 | +2.2 | −6.3 | +8.1 | **54.0** | Approved | Approved ✅ |
| D | 6,000 | 65% | 620 | 0.0 | −12.3 | −5.8 | **31.9** | Rejected | Rejected ✅ |
| E | 11,000 | 25% | 580 | +5.6 | +3.5 | −17.3 | **41.8** | Rejected | Rejected ✅ |

Scenario C by hand:

- Income 8,000 is above the 6,000 reference: n = (8,000 − 6,000) / (15,000 − 6,000) = 0.222 → 10 × 0.222 = **+2.2**
- DTI 48% is above the 30% reference (worse): n = −(48 − 30) / (70 − 30) = −0.45 → 14 × −0.45 = **−6.3**
- Credit 690 is above the 640 reference: n = (690 − 640) / (800 − 640) = 0.3125 → 26 × 0.3125 = 8.125 → **+8.1**
- Score = 50 + 2.2 − 6.3 + 8.1 = **54.0** ≥ 50, DTI 48% ≤ 60% → **Approved**, 4.0 points above the threshold (borderline).

The **direction** of every factor also matches the report's narrative (§8.2 / Table 1): A all
supporting; B and C income and credit supporting, DTI a concern; D DTI and credit concerns, income
neutral; E income and DTI supporting, credit the concern. These are checked by automated tests
(`tests/engine.test.ts`). No scenario is special-cased anywhere in the code.

## 3. Calibration: why these numbers (and the conflict we found)

The brief asked us to check the formula against the report and explain any conflict instead of
forcing outputs. We did that with a parameter search (script kept out of the repository; summary here).

**First attempt — equal weights.** A symmetric policy giving each factor ±16.7 points
(income 3,000/7,000/15,000; DTI 0/30/60; credit 500/650/800; threshold 50) produced:

| A | B | C | D | E |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 88.9 ✅ | 61.3 ✅ | **46.5 ❌ (rejected, report says approved)** | 25.8 ✅ | **53.3 ❌ (approved, report says rejected)** |

**The conflict.** Scenario E has *better* income (11,000 vs 8,000) **and** a much *better* DTI
(25% vs 48%) than Scenario C, yet the report approves C and rejects E. The only factor where E is worse
is credit score (580 vs 690). In any additive scoring policy this is only possible if **a 110-point
credit-score difference outweighs the combined income and DTI differences**. So credit score must
carry the largest weight, and its penalty must grow quickly below the reference point.

**Adjustment.** Credit score was given 26 of the 50 points, with a steeper concern side (550 → 640)
than support side (640 → 800). Income (10) and DTI (14) keep meaningful, monotonic effects, and every
factor can change an outcome on its own (tested).

**Side effect and the DTI limit.** With credit at 26 points, an excellent credit score could fully
offset the worst DTI — e.g. income AED 1,000, DTI 100%, credit 900 would score 52 (−10 − 14 + 26)
and be approved. That is not a defensible result even for a demonstration. We considered three fixes:

1. *Steeper, kinked DTI curve* — reproduces A–E but needs a near-vertical penalty between 60% and 70%,
   which is effectively a hidden cliff and harder to explain.
2. *Different points for support and concern on every factor* — works, but reduces income to ±5 and
   makes the formula much harder to follow.
3. **An explicit, documented affordability limit: DTI above 60% cannot be approved.** ✅ chosen

Option 3 keeps the additive score simple and makes the extra rule visible to the user (results page
alert, explanation text, factor card). It does not change any report scenario's outcome — Scenario D
(65% DTI) is already rejected by its score (31.9) and the limit is shown as an additional reason. The
credit weight (26) is also just above income + DTI (24), so the lowest credit score (300) cannot be
fully offset by the best income and DTI.

**Remaining limitations (honest list).**

- Strong factors can still compensate for weak ones below the limit (e.g. AED 1,000 income with 0% DTI
  and an 800 credit score is approved). That is a property of any additive scorecard.
- The 60% limit is a sharp threshold: 60.0% can be approved, 60.1% cannot.
- Effects are linear between anchors and capped beyond them (e.g. income above AED 15,000 adds nothing
  more). Real affordability is more nuanced (loan size, term, expenses, employment, etc. are ignored).
- The reference points and weights reflect the report's five fictional scenarios, not real data.

## 4. "What would change the outcome"

For rejected applications the engine re-runs the policy changing **one factor at a time** (others held
fixed) and reports the smallest change that reaches approval, e.g. Scenario E: *raise the credit score
from 580 to at least 609* or *lower DTI from 25% to 13.3% or less*. Income is not offered for E because
even the maximum income contribution cannot close the 8.2-point gap. This is a simple, calculated
counterfactual — not financial advice.

## 5. Applying the policy to the project dataset

The policy was **not** recalibrated for the dataset (it has no outcome labels to calibrate against).
Applied to all 32,424 records after conversion (USD × 3.6725, DTI fraction × 100):

| Group | Approved |
|---|---|
| All records | 11,374 / 32,424 (35.1%) |
| No loan (DTI 0%) | 9,858 / 19,429 (50.7%) |
| With a loan | 1,516 / 12,995 (11.7%) |
| With a loan and DTI ≤ 60% | 1,516 / 3,372 (45.0%) |
| DTI above 60% | 0 / 9,623 (0%) — 2,803 of these scored ≥ 50 and were stopped only by the limit |
| Credit score ≥ 640 | 8,730 / 12,481 (69.9%) |
| Credit score < 640 | 2,644 / 19,943 (13.3%) |

The dataset's credit scores average 575 (below the 640 reference) and most borrowers' repayments are
very high relative to income, which is why approval rates are low for borrowers. These figures
describe how the demonstration policy treats the synthetic data, not how real lenders would.

## 6. Changing the policy

Edit `DEMO_POLICY` in `lib/engine/policy.ts` and bump `version`. `assertValidPolicy` rejects
inconsistent settings (e.g. a score range outside 0–100). Then run `npm test`: the scenario tests will
tell you immediately if A–E no longer match the report. Stored applications keep the `policyVersion`,
contributions and explanation they were created with.
